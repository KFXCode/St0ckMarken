👋 How to use this app (read me first!)
What this app is. Every morning a robot looks at hundreds of stocks (and crypto, forex & commodities) and picks a few really good ones it thinks will go up or down. It shows you exactly what to do. Your job is just to copy the trade. Easy!
Step 1 — set your money. At the top in My Money, type how much you're starting with (even $20 is fine) and tap Start. It's pretend money so you can practice with zero risk. You can change it any time with the Add, Take out, or New amount buttons.
Step 2 — pick your market. Use the market dropdown to choose Stocks, Crypto, Forex, Commodities, or Meme coins. Look for the green $100-friendly ✓ tag — those are cheap enough for a small budget.
Step 3 — read the trade. The green box says it in plain English, the blue box shows chart shapes the robot spotted, and "How your money can grow" shows what you could make… and the most you could lose (never more than you pay).
Step 4 — take the trade. Pick how many with −/+, tap Take this trade to add it to your practice money. Ready for real? Pick your broker up top, then tap Open in [your broker] — it copies the order so you paste & buy in your app.
⭐ Golden rules. 1) Only use money you're okay losing. 2) Start with cheap trades. 3) Nothing sells by itself — you must tap Sell to Close in your broker to keep profit. 4) Slow and steady wins — small wins stack into big money over time.
📗 New to options? The plain-English guide
What you're buying. A call is like a coupon: the right to buy 100 shares at a set price (the strike) until a set date (the expiration). A put is the same but it wins when the stock FALLS. The coupon's price is the premium: premium $0.82 = one contract costs $82 (×100). You never have to buy the shares — almost everyone just sells the coupon once it's worth more.
Turn on options first (one time). In your broker: Settings → Options → Enable (Robinhood, Webull) or apply for options approval (Fidelity, Schwab). Level 1–2 approval is all you need to buy calls and puts.
⚠️ It does NOT sell itself. If the premium rises, the profit is only yours when you tap Sell to Close. Every day an option loses a little value (time decay); at expiration an out-of-the-money option becomes worthless — you lose the premium. Sell it yourself before expiry week.
Simple exit plan. Decide before you buy: take profit at +50% to +100%; cut losses at −50%; and be out at least a week before expiration.
