📗 New to options? Start here — the plain-English guide
What you're actually buying. A call is like a coupon: it gives you the right to buy 100 shares at a set price (the strike) until a set date (the expiration). A put is the same but for selling — it gains value when the stock FALLS. The price of the coupon itself is the premium. Premium $0.82 = one contract costs $82 (× 100 shares). You never have to actually buy the 100 shares — almost everyone just sells the coupon itself once it's worth more.
Step 0 — turn on options at your broker (one time). Every app makes you enable options first: find Settings → Options Trading → Enable (Robinhood, Webull) or apply for "options approval" (Fidelity, Schwab). They ask a few questions about experience and income — answer honestly; "Level 1–2" approval is all you need to buy calls and puts. Approval usually takes minutes to a day.
Step-by-step: placing a pick from this report.
1. Open your broker and search the ticker (e.g. SOFI).
2. Tap Trade → Options (not the regular Buy button — that buys shares).
3. Pick the expiration date shown on the card.
4. Scroll the strike list to the strike on the card. Calls and puts are separate tabs — make sure you're on the right one.
5. Choose Buy to Open, quantity 1 contract.
6. Set a limit price at (or a couple cents above) the premium on the card — never use a market order on options.
7. Review: the cost should match the card (premium × 100). Submit.
⚠️ It does NOT sell itself. If the premium rises, nothing happens automatically — the profit is only yours when you tap Sell to Close on that contract. If you never sell: the option loses a little value every single day (time decay), a paper profit can melt back to zero, and at expiration an out-of-the-money option becomes worthless — you lose the whole premium. (If it finishes IN the money, most brokers auto-sell or auto-exercise it on expiration day, but never rely on that — sell it yourself before expiry week.)
A simple exit plan (what most disciplined traders do). Decide before you buy: take profit by selling at +50% to +100%; cut the loss by selling at −50%; and no matter what, be out at least a week before expiration, when time decay is fastest.
